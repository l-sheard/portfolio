Building a job scheduler for my final year project taught me more about distributed systems than any lecture did. A few things that stuck with me:

## Retries are easy. Idempotent retries are not.

The first version of my scheduler retried failed jobs blindly. It took a very confusing afternoon of double-charged mock invoices before I understood why idempotency keys exist. If a job can have side effects, "just retry it" is a design decision, not a default.

## Heartbeats are a trade-off, not a solved problem

Detecting a dead worker quickly means polling often, which costs resources. Polling rarely means slow failure detection. There's no free lunch here — you're choosing a point on a curve, and the right point depends entirely on what you're scheduling.

## Reading real systems is worth more than reading about them

I got further in an afternoon reading Sidekiq's source than I did in a week of blog posts about "how job queues work." If you want to understand a piece of infrastructure deeply, there is no substitute for reading the code of something that survived production.
